Questions Analysis:
------------------
1. Did pixel accuracy decrease?
Yes, the global pixel accuracy decreased. Making the spatial structures smaller directly limits the capacity of our Small U-Net to correctly map every pixel, forcing higher mathematical penalties during CrossEntropyLoss evaluation.

2. Which class became harder?
The Urban (class 3) and Water (class 2) classes became significantly harder. Because their representative widths and areas were cut down drastically, they now occupy a microscopic percentage of the total image, increasing false negative classifications.

3. Did small structures become more difficult?
Yes, small structures became immensely more difficult. Due to the two MaxPool2d pooling layers in the U-Net encoder, an initial 6x6 pixel urban block is compressed down to an intermediate feature map representation that is smaller than a single pixel before reaching the bottleneck. Without massive localized feature representation, the decoder cannot accurately reconstruct their precise spatial coordinates.

4. What does this suggest about real Earth Observation data?
This confirms that in operational Earth Observation systems, spatial resolution and object scale are critical challenges. Real-world target structures like thin roads, small buildings, or narrow drainage streams require specialized high-resolution network configurations. Relying solely on global pixel accuracy is deeply misleading for monitoring rare or highly fragmented classes, since a model can maintain an apparently high score by simply predicting the large, uniform background terrain accurately while completely missing vital small-scale details.
